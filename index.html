Injector flow is used at the source’s calibration, with a pressure multiplier of 1. The source does not specify fuel pressure. Pulse width includes the 0.5 ms delay; the 95% duty cap is inactive at both points. These are fuel-power results, before any coolant fraction or safety factor.

01

Flow from each injector

qref = flow [cc/min] × ρ [g/cc]
q = qref √(Δp / Δpref)

230 cc/min is flow per injector. Density converts volume to mass. The square root corrects for the pressure difference between the fuel rail and intake manifold.

With gauge fuel pressure and absolute MAP: Δp = Pfuel + Patm − MAP.

02

How long it stays open

Dcmd = min(PW × RPM × n / 120000, Dmax)
Deff = max(Dcmd − tdelay × RPM × n / 120000, 0)

PW and delay are in milliseconds. n is the number of pulses per 720° cycle. Delay is subtracted only when it is included in the logged pulse width.

Inherited model: duty is capped before subtracting delay. Delay is constant; actual voltage is unavailable in the included logs.

03

From mass to energy

ṁfuel = Σ (N × q × Deff) / 60
Pfuel [kW] = ṁfuel [g/s] × LHV [MJ/kg]

The sum supports two banks with separate signals. The CP2 preset applies the same PW to both injectors. Fuel power includes energy that becomes mechanical work, exhaust heat and other losses.

04

From fuel energy to coolant heat

Q̇coolant = Pfuel × fcoolant
Q̇with margin = Q̇coolant × SF

25%, 30%, 35% and 40% are sensitivity scenarios. This fraction has not been measured for our engine. 40% explores a higher heat load; it is not a measured WOT fraction.

SF 1.2 is a proposed design margin, not a statistical confidence interval.

What does a time window do?

Fuel mass is integrated using trapezoids between samples. For a 10 s window, the integrated mass is divided by 10 s to find average flow. The calculation repeats at each sample and selects the highest complete window. A 15 s window follows the same process over 15 s.

P̄window = (mwindow / duration) × LHV

Window boundaries are interpolated. Large gaps, invalid rows and time resets split the data into separate segments. Run-average flow is total mass divided by total valid time, not a simple average of rows or windows. The chart reduces points for display only; calculations use every sample.

Why not use ṁ × cₚ × ΔT?

Q̇radiator = ṁcoolant × cp × (Tin − Tout)

This directly checks heat transfer on the coolant side. It requires coolant flow and simultaneous radiator inlet and outlet temperatures. Engine temperature alone is not enough. During transients, the system also stores heat.

What to validate on driving day

  • Compare integrated fuel mass with measured consumption over the same interval.
  • Record radiator inlet and outlet temperatures and measure coolant flow.
  • Record the driver, route, tune, idle time and ambient conditions.
  • Repeat the test and check any model adjustment with an independent run.
